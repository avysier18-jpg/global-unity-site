# NHTSA — MAP-21 Rear-Seat Belt Reminder Rule
**Administrative Mirror Page**

> This page compares statutory requirements, agency statements, administrative record facts, and judicial remedy limits.  
> No individuals are named. No conclusions are asserted.

---

## ① What the Statute Required

**Law:** Moving Ahead for Progress in the 21st Century Act (MAP-21)  
**Responsible Agency:** National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)  
**Subject:** Rear-seat belt reminder systems  

**Statutory Duty:** Issue a **final rule** requiring rear-seat belt reminder systems.  
**Deadline:** **October 2015**  

**Nature of Duty:** Discrete · Mandatory · Date-certain

---

## ② What the Agency Said

Over multiple years following enactment, NHTSA publicly stated that it was:

- Studying rear-seat belt reminder technologies  
- Evaluating safety benefits  
- Considering implementation as part of broader vehicle safety initiatives  

**Public Posture:** ongoing consideration, not completion.

---

## ③ What the Administrative Record Shows

- **Oct 2015:** statutory deadline passed without a final rule  
- **2015–2023:** no final rule issued  
- **Aug 2023:** **Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM)** published  
- **As of filing:** no final rule issued

**Documented facts:** the statutory deadline was missed by nearly a decade; action occurred only after prolonged delay; the record reflects explanation and study, not completion.

---

## ④ What Courts Can / Cannot Compel

**Courts can (APA):**
- Declare agency action **unlawfully withheld** or **unreasonably delayed**
- Remand with instructions to act within a court-set timeframe

**Courts cannot:**
- Design the safety standard
- Dictate technical specifications
- Supervise agency operations beyond ordering action

**Practical effect:** remedies focus on compelling action, not directing policy substance.
