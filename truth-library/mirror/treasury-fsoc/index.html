Treasury — FSOC Risk Assessment

Administrative Mirror page comparing statutory duties, agency statements, administrative record facts, and judicial remedy limits. No individuals are named. No conclusions are asserted.

① What the Statute Required

  • Law
    Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (12 U.S.C. §§ 5321–5323)
  • Subject
    Identification and reporting of risks to U.S. financial stability
  • Responsible agency
    U.S. Department of the Treasury (as Chair of the Financial Stability Oversight Council)
  • Statutory duty
    Identify, assess, and report on risks to the financial stability of the United States, including risks arising from household financial vulnerability
  • Standard
    Agency action must reflect reasoned decision-making under the Administrative Procedure Act
  • Nature of duty
    Ongoing · Analytical · Subject to APA review
The statute requires reasoned evaluation of systemic risks, not reliance on unexamined inputs.

② What the Agency Said

In the 2023 FSOC Annual Report, Treasury stated:

  • U.S. financial system "remains resilient"
  • Identified inflation and household financial vulnerability as systemic risks
  • Relied on CPI and PCE measures as inputs for evaluating household conditions
  • Acknowledged higher interest rates and rising consumer debt as concerns
Public statements acknowledged household vulnerability while relying on standard inflation inputs.

③ What the Administrative Record Shows

  • December 2023: FSOC Annual Report published, identifying inflationary pressures and household vulnerability as key risks
  • Risk inputs: Report relied on CPI/PCE measures, which incorporate substitution and quality-adjustment assumptions
  • Known limitation: CPI/PCE substitution assumptions may undercount non-substitutable essentials (housing, healthcare, insurance, utilities)
  • Omission: Report did not evaluate whether CPI/PCE input assumptions adequately capture cost pressures relevant to household financial stress and systemic risk
  • Cross-agency evidence: Federal data documents persistent divergence between essential household costs and CPI/PCE-based measures
The report acknowledged household vulnerability but did not evaluate whether its measurement inputs were appropriate for the statutory task.

④ What Courts Can / Cannot Compel

Under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA):

Courts can:

  • Declare agency action arbitrary and capricious under 5 U.S.C. § 706(2)(A)
  • Vacate and remand for reasoned reconsideration
  • Require evaluation of omitted risk categories

Courts cannot:

  • Dictate how inflation should be measured
  • Select alternative economic models
  • Resolve macroeconomic policy disputes
Judicial remedies focus on requiring reasoned analysis, not directing methodological choices.
