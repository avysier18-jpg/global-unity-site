DOL — SECURE 2.0 Paper Statement Rule

Administrative Mirror page comparing statutory duties, agency statements, administrative record facts, and judicial remedy limits. No individuals are named. No conclusions are asserted.

① What the Statute Required

  • Law
    SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022, Pub. L. 117-328, Div. T, §338
  • Subject
    Paper benefit statements for retirement plan participants
  • Responsible agency
    Department of Labor (DOL), Employee Benefits Security Administration (EBSA)
  • Statutory duty
    Update 29 C.F.R. § 2520.104b-1(c) to require at least one annual paper benefit statement and initial paper notice for retirement plan participants
  • Deadline
    December 31, 2024
  • Nature of duty
    Discrete · Mandatory · Date-certain
The statute required issuance of a regulatory update, not further study or discretionary consideration.

② What the Agency Said

Following enactment, DOL publicly indicated it was:

  • Soliciting public input via Request for Information (August 2023)
  • Evaluating implementation approaches
  • Considering changes to electronic disclosure safe harbors
Public statements conveyed ongoing consideration, not completion.

③ What the Administrative Record Shows

  • August 2023: Request for Information published in Federal Register
  • October 2023: Comment period closed
  • December 31, 2024: Statutory deadline passed without final rule
  • September 30, 2025: Proposed rule submitted to OMB (RIN 1210-AC27, per industry trade sources)
  • As of filing: No final rule issued; proposed rule text not yet public
The required outcome — a final regulation — has not occurred.

④ What Courts Can / Cannot Compel

Under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA):

Courts can:

  • Declare agency action unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed
  • Order issuance of required regulation by a court-set deadline

Courts cannot:

  • Design the regulation's content
  • Dictate policy choices within the regulation
  • Supervise ongoing agency operations beyond ordering action
Judicial remedies focus on compelling action, not directing policy substance.
