All Government Agencies Connected to Retirement, Housing, Markets, and Financial Systems

Core Federal Agencies (Most Critical)

  1. Federal Reserve (The Fed) — Controls interest rates, creates bank reserves, stabilizes markets during crises
  2. U.S. Department of the Treasury — Issues government bonds, manages national debt
  3. Congress — Writes laws that changed pensions to 401(k)s, controls budgets, creates regulations
  4. Social Security Administration (SSA) — Manages Social Security, invests trust funds in Treasury bonds
  5. Internal Revenue Service (IRS) — Collects taxes, enforces 401(k) rules and tax code
  6. Department of Labor — Oversees ERISA, regulates pensions and 401(k) plans
  7. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) — Calculates inflation (CPI, CPI-W, Chained CPI)
  8. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) — Regulates investments, enforces accredited investor rules

Housing & Financial Crisis Agencies

  1. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) — Housing policy and regulations
  2. Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) — Oversees Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac
  3. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) — Insures banks, stabilizes banking system
  4. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) — Regulates national banks
  5. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) — Consumer financial protection (created post-2008)

Pension & Retirement Oversight

  1. Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation (PBGC) — Insures private pension plans when they fail

Monetary Policy & Crisis Response

  1. Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) — Part of the Fed, sets monetary policy
  2. Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC) — Monitors systemic financial risks (created post-2008)

Courts & Enforcement

  1. Federal Courts (Judiciary) — Enforce compliance, judicial review of regulations, can force agencies to follow laws

Education & Systems Literacy

  1. Department of Education — Federal education policy, though curriculum is often state-controlled
  2. State Education Agencies — Set curriculum standards, implement financial literacy requirements (varies by state)

Executive Branch

  1. The Presidency — Appointments of agency heads (Fed Chair, SEC Chair, etc.), executive orders, enforcement priorities, emergency powers

Why This Matters:

Many people think "the government" is one thing helping or hurting them. In reality:

  • These agencies often have conflicting priorities
  • Some stabilize banks first (Fed, Treasury, FDIC)
  • Some measure inflation in ways that lag real costs (BLS)
  • Some enforce rules that shifted retirement risk to workers (Department of Labor, IRS)
  • Some restrict investment opportunities to the wealthy (SEC)
  • Congress writes the laws that created these systems
  • Courts can force change but only when challenged

The system isn't one entity—it's 20+ agencies with different rules, priorities, and incentives. Understanding which agency controls which part of your financial life helps you know where to direct pressure and who to hold accountable.
