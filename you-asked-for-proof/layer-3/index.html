1. Retirement Risk Was Shifted to Workers

For decades, many workers were promised retirement through employer pensions. Over time, pensions declined, individual retirement accounts replaced them, and workers took on market risk. After major market crashes in 2000 and 2008, employers froze or closed pension plans, and those plans were rarely restored. This shift remained in place.

Proof:

  • GAO — pension freezes and plan terminations
  • SSA — disappearing defined benefit pensions
  • CRS — continued decline of private-sector pensions

2. The Cost of Living Was Measured Lower Than What People Pay

Official inflation measures use housing methods that lag real rent and home price increases. The same measures are still used for wages, benefits, tax brackets, and policy.

Proof:

  • BLS — how housing is measured in inflation
  • Minneapolis Fed — housing inflation can remain elevated
  • U.S. Treasury — housing and rents rising faster than income

3. Housing Rules Turned High Prices Into Long-Term Debt

A federal housing regulator oversees housing finance and mortgage markets. As home prices and rates rose, mortgage costs rose and access tightened, locking people into decades of debt.

Proof:

  • FHFA — mandate and role
  • CRS — rising rates and prices increased homebuying costs

4. Warnings Were Given — Decisions Went Ahead Anyway

Government reports and internal records documented risks before the crisis, yet policy continued. Markets recovered faster than wages, and core structures stayed in place.

Proof:

  • FCIC — crisis was avoidable and warnings were not acted on
  • Fed transcript (Jan 2007) — risks identified pre-crisis

5. Debt & Scarcity Framing

Money is created through administrative processes, while taxes are framed as necessary to “pay for” spending and control demand.

Proof:

  • St. Louis Fed — open market operations
  • Joint Economic Committee — tax policy for stability

6. None of This Was Reversed

After each crisis, harms were acknowledged, but core structures remained. The same pattern repeated over decades.

Proof:

  • FCIC — final report
  • GAO-16-408 — market-based model continued
