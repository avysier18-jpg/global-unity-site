Act 1 — Retirement Risk Shifted to Workers

Before, many long-tenured workers—especially at large employers and unionized workplaces—had pensions that offered more predictable retirement income than today’s system. It was not universal, and it was not perfectly guaranteed, but the big difference was this: if the plan came up short, the company carried most of the shortfall risk, not the worker.

Over time, a mix of policy changes and business incentives reshaped retirement. ERISA (1974) created protections for retirement plans and introduced IRAs. Later tax-code changes—including the Revenue Act of 1978 and 1981 IRS rules—accelerated 401(k)-style salary deferrals. Employers steadily shifted away from pensions toward market-linked retirement accounts, moving more retirement risk onto workers.

Pensions were market-exposed too—but the sponsor carried the funding and shortfall risk. 401(k)s move that residual risk onto workers.

This shift increased portability and reduced employer liability, but it also tied retirement security more tightly to financial markets. If markets crash, workers can lose years of savings—even if they did nothing wrong.

“Over time, employers shifted from pensions to market-linked accounts, moving retirement risk onto workers.”

Act 2 — The Illusion of Control

After pensions declined, workers were told they were now “in control” of their retirement. Many workers are nudged—and increasingly auto-enrolled in larger plans—into deferring part of their paycheck into a 401(k), which is then invested in markets. This money comes from people’s labor—their time, their work, their lives.

Whether markets rise or fall, fees and administrative costs are typically collected regardless of market direction. Workers can change contribution rates, but most workers can’t avoid meaningful market and inflation risk and still build a viable retirement—especially without a pension.

Portability is a real benefit. It helped workers leave bad jobs and keep savings. But portability did not require shifting this much risk onto workers.

“Workers’ retirement savings became dependent on markets, while fees are collected regardless of outcomes.”

Context Note — Risk Was Shifted Without Preparation

As retirement and financial risk shifted from companies to workers, financial literacy was not made a consistent, nationwide requirement… “Financial risk was made mandatory, but financial education was not.”
