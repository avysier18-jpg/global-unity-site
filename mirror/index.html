Mirror

See what agencies said. See what actually happened.

Step 1 of 5

Law / Statute

What the law says the agency must do. We list the exact law, section number, and deadline. This is the rule the agency is required to follow.

Sources: Congress.gov U.S. Code Public Laws Federal Register
Step 2 of 5

Agency Statement

What the agency said publicly about the law. We quote or summarize official statements — plans, explanations, or updates the agency shared. We do not guess or explain meaning.

Sources: Press Releases Regulatory Agendas Official Reports Congressional Testimony
Step 3 of 5

Public Record

What actually happened. We check public records to see if the agency did what the law required. Did they meet the deadline? Did they publish the rule? The record answers those questions.

Sources: Federal Register OMB Dashboards Agency Websites Public Filings
Step 4 of 5

Court Limits

What courts can and cannot force an agency to do. Courts can order agencies to act when the law requires it. Courts cannot write policy or control how agencies decide details.

Sources: Administrative Procedure Act Court Decisions Established Legal Rules

The final output. All four boxes shown together on one page. Every agency uses the same structure. Because the format is always the same, patterns across agencies become easy to see.

What Courts Can / Cannot Compel

Under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA):

Courts can:

  • Declare agency action unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed
  • Remand with instructions to act within a court-set timeframe

Courts cannot:

  • Design the safety standard
  • Dictate technical specifications
  • Supervise ongoing agency operations beyond ordering action
Judicial remedies focus on compelling action, not directing policy substance.

What This Is

The Mirror shows what laws say agencies must do. Then it shows what agencies said. Then it shows what actually happened. Each page uses the same format so you can compare them.

What This Is Not

  • Not a campaign or petition
  • Not naming any person
  • Not guessing why things happened
  • Not scoring agencies as good or bad
  • Not legal advice

We just show the facts. You decide what they mean.

How Each Page Is Built

Every Mirror page has four boxes:

1. Law Said

What the law told the agency to do.

2. Agency Said

What the agency said about it.

3. Record Shows

What actually happened.

4. Courts Can Do

What a judge can or cannot order.

The rule: If a fact does not fit in one box, it does not go on the page.

How Pages Get Updated

  • Each page shows when it was last checked
  • When an agency acts, we update the page
  • Old versions are saved

This is built to last, not just to publish once.

Who Uses This

  • Researchers — compare agencies using the same format
  • Reporters — find sources and citations in one place
  • Teachers — show students how government works
  • Everyone — check facts without being a lawyer
