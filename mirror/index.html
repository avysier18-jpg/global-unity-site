What This Is

The Mirror shows what laws say agencies must do. Then it shows what agencies said. Then it shows what actually happened. Each page uses the same format so you can compare them.

What This Is Not

  • Not a campaign or petition
  • Not naming any person
  • Not guessing why things happened
  • Not scoring agencies as good or bad
  • Not legal advice

We just show the facts. You decide what they mean.

How Each Page Is Built

Every Mirror page has four boxes:

1. Law Said

What the law told the agency to do.

2. Agency Said

What the agency said about it.

3. Record Shows

What actually happened.

4. Courts Can Do

What a judge can or cannot order.

The rule: If a fact does not fit in one box, it does not go on the page.

How Pages Get Updated

  • Each page shows when it was last checked
  • When an agency acts, we update the page
  • Old versions are saved

This is built to last, not just to publish once.

Who Uses This

  • Researchers — compare agencies using the same format
  • Reporters — find sources and citations in one place
  • Teachers — show students how government works
  • Everyone — check facts without being a lawyer

NHTSA — MAP-21 Rear-Seat Belt Reminder Rule

Rulemaking delay

What the Statute Required

MAP-21 required NHTSA to amend FMVSS to require rear-seat belt reminder systems.

Deadline: October 2015

What the Agency Said

NHTSA stated it was studying rear-seat belt reminder technologies and evaluating safety benefits.

What the Record Shows

  • October 2015: Deadline passed without final rule
  • 2015-2023: No final rule issued
  • August 2023: NPRM published
  • As of filing: No final rule issued

What Courts Can/Cannot Compel

Can: Declare action unlawfully withheld; order issuance by court-set deadline.

Cannot: Design safety standard; dictate technical specifications.

DOL — SECURE 2.0 Paper Statement Rule

Rulemaking delay

What the Statute Required

SECURE 2.0 Act §338 required DOL to update regulations to require at least one annual paper benefit statement for retirement plan participants.

Deadline: December 31, 2024

What the Agency Said

DOL indicated it was soliciting public input via RFI (August 2023), evaluating implementation approaches, and considering changes to electronic disclosure safe harbors.

What the Record Shows

  • August 2023: RFI published
  • December 31, 2024: Deadline passed without final rule
  • September 30, 2025: Proposed rule submitted to OMB
  • As of filing: No final rule issued

What Courts Can/Cannot Compel

Can: Declare action unlawfully withheld; order issuance by court-set deadline.

Cannot: Design regulation content; dictate policy choices.

HHS — Statutory Publication Duties

Publication delay

What the Statute Required

FSMA § 110(g): Publish biennial Food Safety and Food Defense Research Plan. ACA § 3011: Submit and publish annual National Health Care Quality Strategy updates.

Deadlines: Biennial (FSMA); Annual (ACA)

What the Agency Said

HHS agencies stated they are coordinating food safety research across HHS, USDA, and DHS, and publishing related reports through AHRQ.

What the Record Shows

  • May 2015: FDA/HHS published biennial report citing FSMA § 110(g)
  • 2017-2023 cycles: No subsequent § 110(g) research plans located
  • National Quality Strategy: Annual updates not consistently published

What Courts Can/Cannot Compel

Can: Declare action unlawfully withheld; order publication by deadline.

Cannot: Dictate content of reports; direct research priorities.

Treasury — FSOC Risk Assessment

Risk-input evaluation

What the Statute Required

Dodd-Frank Act (12 U.S.C. §§ 5321-5323) requires Treasury/FSOC to identify, assess, and report on risks to U.S. financial stability, including household financial vulnerability.

Standard: Reasoned decision-making under APA

What the Agency Said

2023 FSOC Annual Report stated U.S. financial system "remains resilient," identified inflation and household vulnerability as systemic risks, and relied on CPI/PCE measures as inputs.

What the Record Shows

  • December 2023: FSOC Annual Report published
  • Risk inputs: Report relied on CPI/PCE measures with substitution assumptions
  • Omission: Report did not evaluate whether inputs adequately capture household financial stress

What Courts Can/Cannot Compel

Can: Declare action arbitrary/capricious; vacate and remand for reasoned reconsideration.

Cannot: Dictate how inflation should be measured; select economic models.
